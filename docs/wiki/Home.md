# Dr Semmelweis Handwashing Discovery

Day 80 of Udemy's *100 Days of Code*: you become Dr Ignaz Semmelweis, a physician at the Vienna General Hospital in the 1840s, and use data to show that handwashing saved the lives of mothers. This wiki explains what each lecture step teaches. Every step has a picture generated from the code.

| [Step 2: Preliminary data exploration](Step-2-Data-Exploration) | What is in the data, is it clean, and how dangerous was childbirth in the 1840s? |
| [Step 3: The yearly data split by clinic](Step-3-Clinic-Split) | Which clinic was busier, and which one was more dangerous? |
| [Step 4: The effect of handwashing](Step-4-Handwashing-Effect) | Did handwashing lower the proportion of women who died? |

The question of the assignment: did making doctors wash their hands in 1847 change how many women died in childbirth, and is the change more than chance?
